# Meta Trends Analysis: Turbo Decks (May 2026)

This document tracks empirical findings regarding specific archetype performance across tournament size tiers.

## Hypothesis
**"Turbo decks perform better in large-scale tournaments than in locals or small-stakes events."**

## Methodology
- **Sample Size**: 5,792 tournament entries.
- **Commanders Analyzed**: RogSi, DargoTymna, Etali, Ral, Inalla, K'rrik.
- **Tiers**:
  - Locals (<32 players)
  - Mid (32-63 players)
  - Large (64-127 players)
  - Major (128+ players)
- **Metric**: Relative Conversion Rate (Actual % / Expected %). Expected % is `16 / player_count`.

## Findings

### 1. Top 16 Conversion Density
The data confirms that for "proactive" or "turbo" strategies, the primary indicator of success in large fields is **Top 16 conversion**, not necessarily an increased raw win rate.

| Archetype | Large (64-127) T16 Rel. | Major (128+) T16 Rel. |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **RogSi** | 2.0x | 2.0x |
| **DargoTymna** | 2.0x | 0.7x |
| **Ral, Monsoon** | 1.0x | 0.7x |

### 2. Win Rate Stability
Absolute win rates for these archetypes tend to slightly decrease as tournament size increases (averaging ~18-22% in Majors vs ~23-25% in Locals). This is likely due to the higher volume of interaction and longer swiss rounds inherent to larger fields.

### 3. The "Swiss Spread" Advantage
Turbo decks "punch up" significantly in the 64-127 player range. They maintain a **2x expected conversion rate**, suggesting they are highly effective at navigating the early rounds of a larger field where they can capitalize on varied deck optimization levels to secure a high seed.

## Conclusion
The hypothesis is **partially true**. Turbo decks (especially RogSi) are disproportionately represented in the Top 16 of large events relative to their entry count. However, this is a "Top 16 density" advantage rather than a raw win-rate advantage.
